After the jobs

Job 4 ended with a plant drinking brine at the edge of buried Martian ice. Three things are left to say. What could sink this. How unfinished it honestly is. And why it is still worth writing down.

What could sink this design

Worst first, and the first two are worse than anything in versions 2, 3 or 4.

The risk Why it matters
1 Drying out every single night, for years Resurrection plants dry out occasionally, a few times a year. Version 5 asks for several hundred times a year, for the life of the plant. Nobody has measured whether anything survives that, and nothing on Earth does it
2 Breathing only at night has never been finished Moving the cactus way of taking in carbon into another plant is something international research groups have worked on for years without completing it. Version 5 needs it
3 The plant may be dormant nearly all the time It works only below freezing, only in daylight, and only where ice is shallow. Those three conditions may overlap for very few hours a year, and nobody has counted them
4 Warmth is needed and forbidden at the same time The roots want warmth to reach brine. The leaves must stay cold or they boil. Whether one plant can be both has no answer here
5 No site has everything Thick air is in Hellas basin. Shallow ice is at high latitudes. Warm ground is in between. The design wants all three and Mars offers them separately
6 It could be version 1’s mistake again Version 1 stacked 37 genes and the simulation destroyed most of it. Version 5 stacks fewer, but this project has been wrong about exactly this shape of design before

How unfinished this is, said plainly

Version 5 is the least finished thing in this project and it should not be read as a design. It is a direction with some physics under it.

Versions 2, 3 and 4 Version 5
Could a lab start on it now yes only two of its questions
Are the hard parts solved anywhere on Earth yes two of four, no
Does it produce food yes no, and probably never
Has it been modelled yes the physics, yes. The biology, no

We are publishing it because the project has always published what it could not make work, and because the physics finding inside it is worth having on its own.

What is genuinely worth keeping

Even if no plant is ever built this way, three things came out of version 5 that stand by themselves.

What we found Why it matters
A leaf on Mars boils, it does not wilt Above freezing, the water inside a leaf is at a higher pressure than the whole Martian atmosphere. That single fact rules out every warm open-air plan for growing on Mars, and it is arithmetic anyone can check
Thin air is carbon-rich air Martian air offers a leaf nearly fourteen times more carbon dioxide than Earth’s does, and lets it move 166 times faster. Everyone treats the thin atmosphere as pure disadvantage. For a leaf, half of it is an advantage
The cold is the design, not the obstacle Put those two together and the conclusion inverts. A Martian plant does not survive in spite of the cold. It can only work at all because it is cold

And one warning we nearly walked into

While working out the water sums, the model told us that a leaf at twenty degrees uses water more efficiently on Mars than on Earth. The arithmetic was right. The number was meaningless, because above freezing the water is not diffusing out at all, it is boiling out, and boiling was not in the equation.

A number can be correct and physically nonsense at the same time. That is the third time a model has nearly fooled this project, and it is the reason every version has a page like this one.

The one line

Everything that makes Mars hostile to a plant comes from one number. The air is six millibars. You cannot beat it, and the only plants that could ever live out there are the ones cold enough not to need to.
