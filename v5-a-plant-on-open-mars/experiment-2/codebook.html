Codebook · V5 · Exp 2 · Can a plant dry out and revive, night after night?

V5 is A plant on open Mars, and this is one of its two experiments. Every sheet, every column.

Four spreadsheets hold this experiment, printed and filled in by hand at the bench, then typed up exactly as written. This page says what belongs in every column. Write down what you measured and what you saw, and nothing else. Nothing is averaged at the bench.

The twenty four trays

Four cycling rates, six trays each. Each rate gets three trays of desert moss and three of a resurrection plant, because the two may not behave the same way at all.

Rate How often it dries and rewets Trays What it answers
A daily T01 to T06 what Mars would actually demand
B weekly T07 to T12 about fifty cycles in a year
C monthly T13 to T18 twelve cycles, close to what a wild plant does
D never, kept damp throughout T19 to T24 what plain ageing looks like over the same year

Rate D is the control that matters. Plants get old and die on their own, and without D a decline in rate A would prove nothing.

The rates are a ladder, not a pair. If drying damage adds up per cycle then A fails first and C much later. If it adds up with time instead, all four fall together, and the drying is not what is doing it.

Set the trays out in the shuffled bench_position order from trays.csv, never grouped by rate, and label them by number only. The key stays with this sheet until the year is finished.

Every tray gets the same light. A tray in better light recovers better, and that would be mistaken for the cycling.

Do not throw a failed tray away. Some plants recover after looking dead for weeks, and a discarded tray cannot show that.

The four sheets

Here is the whole set at a glance. One sheet carries the answer. The others are there to make that answer worth believing.

The sheet What it is When you fill it in
trays.csv the register, saying which tray dries how often once, after the trays have settled
cycles.csv one row for every drying, and how long rewetting took every time a tray dries
recovery.csv the fluorometer reading, which says how well the plant still works. The answer comes from here once a month, two hours after rewetting
care_log.csv the monthly check, and anything that went wrong once a month

trays.csv

The register. Filled in once when the trays have settled, and never changed. Let all twenty four settle for a month before any cycling starts, so the starting point is a healthy plant and not a shipped one.

level A to D

date_started YYYY-MM-DD, the day cycling began
notes anything unusual about the tray

cycles.csv

One row for every dry and wet cycle of every tray. The trays on rate D never dry, so they have no rows here at all.

Download cycles.csv · blank, ready to fill in

Column What goes in it
tray_id which tray
cycle 1, 2, 3 and on. The count that this whole experiment is about
day days since cycling began
date YYYY-MM-DD
dry_mass_g the weight the tray reached before rewetting, to 0.01 g. Dry to a weight, never to a time, so that every cycle is the same
rewet_minutes how long the tray took to drink back to its starting weight. A tired plant takes longer
notes anything seen: a tray that dried unevenly, one that got watered twice

recovery.csv

The monthly fluorometer round, on every tray, two hours after rewetting. This is the sheet that answers the question.

Download recovery.csv · blank, ready to fill in

Column What goes in it
tray_id which tray
month 1 to 12
day days since cycling began
date YYYY-MM-DD
cycles_done how many cycles this tray has been through by today. Copy it from cycles.csv and count, never from memory
fv_fm what the fluorometer said. It runs from 0 to about 0.8, and a healthy plant reads about 0.76. It says whether photosynthesis restarted, which is the real question. A plant can look green and be dead
hours_after_rewet hours between rewetting and the reading. It should be 2 every time
method which instrument, and how long the plant was dark adapted
notes anything unusual: a repeated reading, a tray still visibly wet

If there is no fluorometer, photograph every tray under fixed lighting and measure colour instead. Put the colour number in fv_fm, say so in method, and say plainly in the report that it is a proxy.

The monthly round. Between cycles the trays just sit there, and this is the only thing that touches them.

The sheets with invented numbers in them

The same four sheets also exist filled in by a script, so they can be checked before there is any real data. This set is the expected outcome: recovery falls with the number of cycles a tray has been through rather than with the time it has sat on the shelf. The daily trays run out of cycles first and fail inside the year. The weekly trays sag. The monthly trays barely move. The trays that never dried only age slowly.

A blank cell is not a zero

A blank means not measured. A 0 means measured, and there was none. Those are different facts. If a tray was not read that month, leave the cell empty. Never type a 0 to fill the gap.
