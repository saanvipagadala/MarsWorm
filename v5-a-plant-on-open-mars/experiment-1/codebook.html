Codebook · V5 · Exp 1 · Does a cold leaf hold its water at Martian pressure?

V5 is A plant on open Mars, and this is one of its two experiments. Every sheet, every column.

Five spreadsheets hold this experiment, printed and filled in by hand at the bench, then typed up exactly as written. This page says what belongs in every column. Write down what you measured and what you saw, and nothing else. Nothing is averaged at the bench.

The ninety leaves

A ladder of five temperatures, three leaves at each step. That is fifteen leaves in one run. The whole ladder is climbed six times: once for each of three leaf types, at each of two pressures. Ninety leaves in all.

Held at What the physics predicts at 6 millibars
-20 C slow, steady water loss
-10 C slow, steady water loss
-2 C slow, still below the line
+5 C above the line. Rapid, visible loss
+20 C far above the line. Very rapid loss

The point of the ladder is to find a step, not a slope. If water simply leaves faster when it is warmer, that is ordinary drying and this experiment has nothing to say. If the change between -2 C and +5 C is far bigger than any other change on the ladder, that is boiling, and that is the result.

The same ladder is climbed at two pressures. One is Mars, the other is this room.

Pressure What it is for
6 mbar Martian surface pressure. This is the condition being tested
1013 mbar ordinary room pressure. The control, so that what the cold alone does can be separated from what the pressure does

Three kinds of leaf climb it. They are picked to be as different from one another as leaves get.

Leaf type Why it is in
crop leaf an ordinary radish or lettuce leaf, the plant version 4 works on
desert succulent a thick waxy skin, the thing most likely to move the line
moss thin and small, with the least to hold its water back

Set the leaves out in the shuffled bench_position order from samples.csv, never grouped by temperature, and label them by number only. The key stays with this sheet, so whoever weighs the leaves does not know which is which.

Bring each leaf to its temperature before pumping down. A leaf that was still warming up while the chamber emptied would show a change that came from thawing, not from pressure.

The five sheets

Here is the whole set at a glance. One sheet carries the answer. The others are there to make that answer worth believing.

The sheet What it is When you fill it in
samples.csv the register, saying which leaf was held at which temperature and pressure once, on the day you cut the leaves
mass_log.csv the weight of the leaf as the hour goes by. The answer comes from here every few minutes, all through the run
dry_mass.csv the dried leaf, which says how much water there was to lose once, after the run
recovery.csv whether the leaf came back after being rewetted once, two days later
run_log.csv the chamber itself, so a run that drifted can be spotted once for each of the six runs

mass_log.csv

The hour itself. One row for every weighing of every leaf. This is the sheet that answers the question.

Download mass_log.csv · blank, ready to fill in

Column What goes in it
sample_id which leaf
minute 0, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40 or 60, minutes since the chamber reached pressure
clock_time the time on the wall clock, so a slipped weighing can be found later
mass_g the mass on the balance, to 0.001 g
appearance what the leaf looks like, in plain words. bubbling, frothing, collapsed, firm
notes anything seen: a leaf that shifted, a weighing that had to be repeated

Minute 0 and minute 2 are the two that matter most. The loss rate the report uses is worked out from those two weighings and the dry mass. Never skip either one.

The sheets with invented numbers in them

The same five sheets also exist filled in by a script, so they can be checked before there is any real data. This set is the expected outcome: at 6 millibars the three cold steps lose water slowly and at about the same speed, then the rate jumps many times over between -2 C and +5 C and the leaves bubble and collapse. At ordinary pressure the same ladder only slopes.

A blank cell is not a zero

A blank means not measured. A 0 means measured, and there was none. Those are different facts. If a leaf was not weighed at that minute, leave the cell empty. Never type a 0 to fill the gap.
