Would your lab have room for two experiments?

Version 5 has four requirements and two of them are open research problems that we are not going to solve. So we are only asking about the two that can be tested now. Both of them can kill the design cheaply. That is the best thing an experiment can do.

This is an entry for the 3M Young Scientist Challenge 2027.

What we are asking for
Bench space A shelf, and access to a vacuum chamber for one of the two
Containment BSL-1. No organisms beyond ordinary plants, no chemicals, nothing regulated
What is not needed No genetic modification, no Mars chamber, no cold room colder than an ordinary freezer
Student on site A short session a week

The two experiments

Experiment What it settles Cost
1 Does a cold leaf hold its water at Martian pressure? The physics claim the whole design rests on Low
2 Can a plant dry out and revive, night after night? The largest unknown in version 5 Very low

Start with experiment 1, because it is one afternoon

Experiment 1 needs a vacuum chamber, a cold plate, a scale and some leaves.

We calculated that a leaf above freezing on Mars does not wilt, it boils, because the water vapour inside it is at a higher pressure than the whole Martian atmosphere. If that is right, a leaf held at Martian pressure should behave completely differently either side of zero degrees. Cold, it should hold on. Warm, it should lose water violently and visibly.

Nobody needs Mars to test that. A pump gets a jar to six millibars in minutes. The result is either a clean confirmation of the physics or a sign that something about real leaves changes the answer, and either one is worth having before another year goes into this.

Experiment 2 costs almost nothing but time

Experiment 2 is trays of desert moss and a timer.

Our design has the plant dry out completely every single night. Real resurrection plants and desert mosses do this a handful of times a year. Nobody has asked one to do it three hundred times a year, and nobody has measured what happens when they try.

It needs no instruments beyond a scale and a way to measure whether the plant is still photosynthesising. It needs a year. It is the cheapest experiment in this project and the one most likely to end version 5.

What we would do with the results

Every experiment in this project is written up before it is run, with its comparisons and its verdict rules fixed in advance, so nothing can be decided after the fact. A negative result is written up exactly as carefully as a positive one. That has already happened three times in this project, and every time it changed the design for the better.

Version 5 is the version we most expect to be told is wrong.
