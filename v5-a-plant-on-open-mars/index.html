MarsWorm: a plant on open Mars

Where version 4 left off

Version 4 put a plant straight into raw Martian dirt, with no soil made first, by giving the plant the one enzyme that stops it poisoning itself. It works on paper and it grows food. It does all of that inside a dome, where there is already pressure, warmth and light.

A dome is the size of the room it stands in. A settlement needs fields.

What version 5 is

Version 5 takes version 4’s plant outside. It is the last unanswered question in the project, first written down in version 2 as track C: growing on the open Martian surface, with no building of any kind.

Everything version 4’s plant already does still applies. Martian dirt outside is the same dirt, with the same chlorine salts and the same locked-up rock. Version 5 only has to add what the open surface takes away. That is four things: pressure, warmth, water, and shelter from ultraviolet light.

What the physics says, before any biology

We could not test this with the kind of model the rest of the project uses, because whether water stays inside a leaf is physics rather than chemistry. So version 5 needed a second model, and it gave a sharper answer than we expected.

A leaf on Mars boils. Not evaporates. Boils. The air inside a leaf is saturated with water vapour, and on Mars that vapour is at a higher pressure than the entire atmosphere outside.

Where A leaf’s water starts boiling at
Earth, sea level 99 °C
Hellas basin, the deepest place on Mars 9 °C
Mars, average surface 0 °C

So a plant on Mars cannot be warm and open at the same time. Above freezing, every pore it opens is a hole in a pressure vessel.

Below freezing, though, Mars starts to help. Its air holds nearly fourteen times more carbon dioxide than Earth’s, and gases move much faster through thin air. So a cold leaf can take in all the carbon it needs through a tiny opening, held open for a very short time.

That was the aha moment. Version 5 is not a plant that survives the cold. It is a plant that only works because it is cold.

The four jobs

The job What the open surface takes away Status
1 Keep the water in Air pressure. A warm leaf boils, so the plant must work cold and almost sealed Designed. The genes exist. The cold limit is physics and cannot be engineered away
2 Survive the night Warmth. Nights reach -80 °C On paper. Antifreeze proteins buy a few degrees, nowhere near enough. The plant has to dry out instead
3 Block the light that harms Ozone. Ultraviolet reaches the ground unfiltered Designed. Plants already make screening pigments, and this is the easiest of the four
4 Reach the water Rain, rivers and damp soil. The only water is frozen underground On paper. Ties the plant to the few places where ice is shallow

What version 5 does not claim

This is the least finished design in the project and we would rather say so than dress it up.

  • It is not food. A plant that works only below freezing, only in daylight, and only where ice is shallow will grow slowly and yield little. Version 5 is about whether life can hold on outside, not about dinner.
  • Two of its four jobs are unsolved anywhere on Earth. Making a plant breathe only at night, and making a crop dry out completely and revive, are both things that whole research consortia have been working on for years without finishing.
  • It could repeat version 1’s mistake. Version 1 stacked 37 genes into one design and the simulation destroyed most of it. Version 5 stacks fewer, but the shape of the risk is the same, and this project has been wrong about that shape before.

What version 5 is asking for

  • Ask 1: read it and say where it falls down. About 30 minutes, with two specific questions. Design review.
  • Ask 2: room in a lab for the two experiments that can be done now. Lab request.

The cheapest experiment does not need Mars at all. A jar, a vacuum pump and a leaf will show whether real leaves survive Martian pressure at the temperatures the physics says they must hold. That is a bench afternoon, and it would tell us whether version 5 is worth another year.
