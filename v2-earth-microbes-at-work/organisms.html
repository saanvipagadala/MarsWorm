The organisms, and the question for each

Track A uses three ordinary Earth microbes and an earthworm, all orderable, none modified. Each was chosen because it already does its job somewhere on Earth, and each has a place in the five steps described end to end. Each also has one question nobody has answered yet.

Where What lives there What it does
The rock bin Bacillus megaterium gives off a weak acid that frees the phosphorus and iron locked in the rock
The lit tank Rhodobacter sphaeroides 2.4.1 makes food out of air, hydrogen and light
The dark tank Azospira suillum PS eats the perchlorate, breathes the oxygen it gets
The bed Eisenia fetida, red wigglers rots it all down into soil

The question for each one

Bacillus megaterium, the rock bin

Gardeners buy it by the bag to free phosphorus in ordinary flower beds. On Moon dust it freed phosphorus and iron and tripled a plant’s growth. Nobody has ever grown it in Martian regolith simulant. The washed regolith still holds a little of the perchlorate, and this is the one microbe that has to sit on the rock itself.

Will it free the phosphorus and iron from Mars rock, with that poison still in it?

Rhodobacter sphaeroides, the lit tank

It builds itself out of air, hydrogen and light, fixes nitrogen while it does so, and makes no oxygen, which is what keeps the nitrogen fixing alive. The whole chain rests on it pushing out sour acids as it grows, so the dark tank has something to eat. The model says it does. No one has measured a real, unbred strain doing it at a useful rate. It also cannot make two of its own vitamins, and it has never met perchlorate.

Will a plain strain leak enough acid to feed the dark tank, and can it stand the traces of poison the water may still carry?

Azospira suillum, the dark tank

It already eats perchlorate for a living, in water treatment plants on Earth, and the hydrogen-fed version of that reactor already cleans drinking water. It has never been run on Martian wash water, which carries the salts and metals that came out of the rock along with the poison, at Martian levels.

Will it keep destroying perchlorate in that water, fed on hydrogen and the lit tank’s acids, and how much salt can it stand before it slows?

Eisenia fetida, the bed

Red wiggler compost worms, the kind sold for a kitchen bin. They are the only thing here that makes soil. The tanks make clean dust and dead bacteria, and the earthworms and the microbes they carry turn that into something a radish can root in.

Nobody has measured either of these. They are Experiment 2 and Experiment 3, and they are the cheapest things in the project.

How much perchlorate can an earthworm stand, and does a worm eating Mars dust free any phosphorus?
