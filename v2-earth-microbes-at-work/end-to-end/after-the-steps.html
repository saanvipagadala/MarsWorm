After the steps

Step 5 ended with soil in the bed. Three things are left to say. What happens to the water once the soil is made. What we do not yet know, and could still sink this design. And what Track A gave up, and gained, compared with the bioengineered organism of version 1.

The water goes round again

  • Clean water leaves the dark tank in step 4 into a holding tank with three pipes off it. Most goes back to the wash in step 1. A little goes to the lit tank in step 3, carrying the minerals that came out of the regolith. A little waters the bed in step 5.
  • Nothing harmful builds up in it, because the perchlorate is destroyed and the salt is taken out. So the same water can go round for ever.
  • Water is the one thing on Mars you cannot afford to throw away.

What could sink this design

None of this has been grown. It is chemistry that balances in a simulation, plus organisms that do each job somewhere on Earth. Worst first:

The risk Why it matters
1 Nobody has tested whether Rhodobacter can stand any perchlorate If the wash water is ever not fully cleaned, nobody knows what happens to the nitrogen fixing in step 3
2 Nobody has grown either bacterium in Mars regolith Other microbes grown in Mars simulant kept only about a tenth of their normal growth
3 The lit tank has to leak its acids The simulation says Rhodobacter does. No one has measured a real strain. If it holds its carbon, Azospira in the dark tank starves
4 Metals build up in the bed and nothing takes them out A sealed habitat has no river. The bed needs its metals measured, and one day a way to bleed them off. Not designed yet
5 Two things we cannot check from Earth How much chlorate Mars holds, because instruments cannot tell it from perchlorate, so no soak should ever be skipped. And how long the membranes last, since they come from Earth

What we lost

Why
The one-organism story A single microbe doing everything is a better story than a room with two tanks in it
Half the jobs, unsolved Thirteen of the 23 jobs were about surviving outdoors. Inside a dome none are needed. We stepped around them, not through them
Working in the ground Light does not reach into regolith, so Rhodobacter has to live in a tank. Track A cannot be scattered on Mars
Most of the gene design work The 37 genes were the heart of version 1. Track A barely uses them. That work is not wrong, it is just not the answer to this question

What we gained

Why
It might actually work Every piece exists and can be ordered today
It destroys the perchlorate while building the soil Washing alone only moves it. Heating the regolith destroys it but leaves dead mineral, not soil
It closes the water loop On Mars that matters more than efficiency
It puts the earthworms at the centre In version 1 they were a warm-up experiment. Here they are the step the whole design rests on, and the cheapest thing to test
It found out why version 1 fails No known organism can grow unaided on the Mars surface. The blocker is not cold or radiation. It is that there is no liquid water

The one line

The dark tank makes the regolith safe. The lit tank makes the ingredients. The earthworms turn them into soil. Only the last of the three grows a radish, and it cannot happen without the other two.
