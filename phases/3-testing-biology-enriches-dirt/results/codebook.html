The codebook: every sheet, every column

This is the codebook for Stage 3. It lists the four sheets the experiment is written on, names the fifteen cups, and says exactly what belongs in every column of every sheet, along with the one rule about leaving a box empty.

Stage 3 sets up fifteen cups and measures them every week for seven weeks. All of those numbers land here, in four spreadsheets filled in by hand. A column that means one thing at the bench and another in the analysis would quietly ruin the experiment, so each one is written down once, here, and both the person and the code follow it.

Write down only what you can read off an instrument: a weight, a number on a test strip, a count of sprouts. The analysis works out every percentage and average afterwards, so each figure in the report leads back to something somebody actually measured.

The five groups

Three identical cups of each recipe, so 15 cups in all. No single lucky cup can decide anything.

Group What is in the cup Why it is here
1 Regolith only MGS-1 simulant, nothing added the floor: what Mars gives you
2 Regolith + organics MGS-1 + pre-composted leaves and scraps, no worms controls for the food itself: does the organic matter alone do it?
3 Regolith + organics + worms MGS-1 + organics + 3 Eisenia fetida the real thing: live worms doing the work
4 Regolith + organics + castings MGS-1 + organics + harvested castings, no live worms worm poop with no worm in it: is the living worm needed at all?
5 Potting soil ordinary potting soil the ceiling: real soil, for scale

Set the cups out in the shuffled bench_position order from containers.csv, never grouped by recipe. Lined up by recipe, one group quietly gets the brightest, warmest end of the shelf, and nothing done later can undo that.

The four sheets

containers.csv

Before anything can be measured, the fifteen cups have to be told apart. This sheet is that register: which recipe is in each cup, and where it sits on the shelf. It is filled in once on day one and never changed, because every other sheet names a cup by the id written here.

Column What goes in it
container_id C01 to C15, written on the cup in marker
group 1-5
group_name the recipe
bench_position 1-15, left to right on the shelf, in the shuffled order

weekly_measurements.csv

For four weeks the cups just sit there while the worms work. This sheet is how that is watched: five readings from every cup, every week, each one a different way of asking whether dirt is turning into soil. Crumbs left on the sieve, water held in a scoop, pH, nitrate, and how much of a buried cotton square has rotted away.

Column What goes in it
container_id which cup
week 1-4
date YYYY-MM-DD, the day you measured
crumbs_on_sieve_g of a dried 10 g scoop, grams left on the 2 mm sieve
dry_weight_g weight of the dry scoop (aim for 10.0)
wet_weight_g same scoop after watering it and letting it drip
ph number from the pH strip
nitrate_ppm number from the aquarium nitrate strip
cloth_start_g weight of the clean cloth square before burying it
cloth_end_g weight after digging it up, rinsing and drying it
notes anything you noticed: smell, mould, worms escaping

plant_measurements.csv

At week four the soil-making stops and the growing starts. This sheet follows the seedlings from sowing to harvest: how many of the ten seeds are up, and how tall they have got, every two or three days. It is what shows a cup pulling ahead, or dying, while it is still happening.

harvest.csv

Day 21 ends the experiment. This sheet holds the number everything else was leading to: the sprouts from each cup, cut at soil level, dried and weighed. One line per cup, and the whole verdict rests on that column.

A blank cell is not a zero

A blank means not measured. A 0 means measured, and it was zero. Those are different facts, the analysis treats them differently, and it reports how many of each it found. So if you did not measure something, leave it empty. Never type a 0 to fill the gap.
