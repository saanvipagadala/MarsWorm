The codebook: every sheet, every column

This is the codebook for Stage 6. It lists the five sheets the experiment is written on, names the 18 cups, and says exactly what belongs in every column, along with the one rule about leaving a box empty.

Stage 6 puts MarsWorm into Martian dirt and finds out whether a seed will grow in it. Those measurements land here, in five spreadsheets filled in by hand. A column that means one thing at the bench and another in the analysis would quietly ruin the experiment, so each one is written down once, here, and both the person and the code follow it.

Write down only what you can count or weigh: a number off a strip, a count of sprouts, a weight. The analysis works out every average and comparison afterwards, so each figure in the report leads back to something somebody measured.

The six recipes

The recipe What is in it Why it is here
1 Mars dirt only MGS-1 simulant, nothing added the floor: what Mars gives you
2 Mars dirt + food MGS-1 + pre-composted leaves and scraps, nothing alive controls for the food itself: does the organic matter alone do it?
3 Mars dirt + food + earthworms MGS-1 + food + 10 Eisenia fetida the benchmark: Stage 3’s best result, repeated here
4 Mars dirt + food + MarsWorm MGS-1 + food + MarsWorm the treatment: does the engineered microbe beat a worm?
5 Mars dirt + MarsWorm, no food MGS-1 + MarsWorm, nothing else added at all the crux: the only group that could work on a real Mars
6 Potting soil ordinary potting soil the ceiling: real soil, for scale

3 identical cups per recipe = 18 cups, 10 seeds in each.

Group 5 is the one that matters most

Every other way of enriching dirt begins by adding something that was already alive: compost, leaves, scraps. On Mars there is none of that. MarsWorm is built to make its own food out of the air and its own fertiliser out of nitrogen, so group 5 is the only recipe here that could work on a planet where nothing has ever died.

If group 5 fails and group 4 succeeds, MarsWorm is an expensive earthworm.

Why earthworms are in the experiment

Group 3 is the benchmark. Stage 3 already showed earthworms enrich this dirt, so beating nothing proves very little. The question is whether MarsWorm beats a worm, and, in group 5, whether it does so with no food at all.

Why nitrate is measured every week

Nitrate is fertiliser, and it is the one measurement an earthworm cannot fake. A worm moves nitrogen around; it cannot make any. If nitrate climbs in group 5, where nothing was added, it was pulled out of the air, which is the single clearest sign the engineering worked.

Positions on the shelf are shuffled

A shelf has a warm end and a bright end. Lining all the MarsWorm cups up at one end would quietly turn the shelf into the result, so bench_position scatters the recipes and you arrange the cups in that order, not in group order.

The build has to finish first

build_log.csv records the six rounds of Stage 5. Any of them can fail, and round 3 is a genuine bet, because nobody has ever put oxygen-making genes into this microbe. Until all six rounds pass there is no MarsWorm to plant, and the analyser will refuse to report a growth trial rather than quietly describe one run with a half-built organism.

The five sheets

build_log.csv

Before a single cup is filled, this sheet records whether there is anything to plant. One row per Stage 5 round: whether the colonies grew, whether they glowed, whether the DNA check found the cassette, and whether the round’s own test passed. If it does not say yes six times, the growth trial does not happen.

weekly_measurements.csv

Three readings from every cup, once a week for four weeks. Nitrate, because it is fertiliser and an earthworm cannot make any. Crumbs left on the sieve, because soil clumps and dust does not. And pH, because a plant has to be able to stand the chemistry.

A blank cell is not a zero

A blank means not measured. A 0 means measured, and it was zero: nothing sprouted at all. Those are different facts, the analysis treats them differently, and it reports how many of each it found. So if you did not check a cup, leave it empty. Never type a 0 to fill the gap.
