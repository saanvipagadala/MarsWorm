The codebook: every sheet, every column

This is the codebook for Stage 4. It lists the two sheets the experiment is written on, names the 16 conditions and the 48 plates, and says exactly what belongs in every column, along with the one rule about leaving a box empty.

Stage 4 dries out half its worms, stresses all of them, and counts who is left. Those counts land here, in two spreadsheets filled in by hand. A column that means one thing at the microscope and another in the analysis would quietly ruin the experiment, so each one is written down once, here, and both the person and the code follow it.

Write down only what you can count: how many worms went on a plate, how many were alive a day later, and a 0, 1 or 2 four days after that. The analysis works out every survival percentage afterwards, so each figure in the report leads back to something somebody actually counted.

The sixteen conditions

The animal is Caenorhabditis elegans, a transparent soil roundworm a millimetre long. Two things are changed on purpose, whether the worm was dried out and whether its drying gene is broken, and each of those four combinations meets four conditions.

The choices
Strain N2 Normal worm
Strain mutant Worm with its drying gene broken
Drying dried Dried out slowly first
Drying damp Kept damp the whole time
Stress none No stress at all, the control
Stress uv UV light
Stress freeze Two hours in a −20 °C freezer
Stress mars-dust Living on MGS-1 Mars dust

2 strains × 2 drying × 4 stresses = 16 conditions, each done 3 times = 48 plates, with about 50 worms on each.

Two of those choices are worth explaining, because both look like extra work until the experiment goes wrong without them.

A run is the replicate, not a plate. Everything in one run came from the same batch of eggs on the same day, so two plates inside a run are not independent of each other. The report compares runs, which is why there are 3 of everything and not fifty.

The no-stress group is not optional. Drying a worm out is rough treatment by itself. Without worms that are dried and then simply left alone, a drop in survival could mean the drying hurt them just as easily as the stress hurt them, and nothing in the data could tell those apart.

The two sheets

plates.csv

Every plate that goes through the experiment gets one line here, and this is the sheet the answer comes out of. It says which strain was on it, whether it was dried, what stress it met, how many worms went on, and how many were still alive a day later. Everything else on this page exists to make those two counts trustworthy.

Column What goes in it
plate_id P01 … P48, written on the lid

worms_started how many worms went on the plate (aim for 50)
worms_alive_24h how many were moving 24 hours later
recovery_96h 4 days later: 0 = no new worms, 1 = a few, 2 = plate crowded
notes anything odd: contamination, plate dried out, miscount

chamber_log.csv

The drying is the one thing being done on purpose, so it has to be proved rather than assumed. Once per run this sheet records what the chamber really did: how far the humidity came down, over how long, and at what temperature. It is the evidence that the dried and damp worms differed in exactly one way.

Why there is no egg count

Alive is not the same as well, so the obvious next question is how many eggs each survivor lays. Nobody can do that here. Counting eggs properly means picking every single worm onto its own plate and moving it to a fresh one every day for about five days: hundreds of plates, and more work than the rest of this experiment put together.

So recovery_96h asks the cheap version of the same question. Look at the plate once, four days later, and score whether there is a next generation on it. It is a rough three-point score, it cannot say how much healthier one group is, and the report says so on the chart. But a group that survived well and still scores 0 is worth a second look, and that is the whole job.

A blank cell is not a zero

A blank means not counted. A 0 means counted, and it was zero: every worm on that plate died. Those are different facts, the analysis treats them differently, and it reports how many of each it found. So if you did not count a plate, leave it empty. Never type a 0 to fill the gap.
