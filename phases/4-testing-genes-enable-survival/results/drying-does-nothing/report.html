What we found out

Each question below was answered by the same rule, written down before anyone looked at the numbers, so a disappointing answer counts exactly as much as a good one.

Does drying a worm first help it survive?

No

Drying the worms first made no difference to survival.

Is the drying gene the reason?

Nothing to compare

Drying did not protect the worms here, so there is no protection for this gene to explain.

Is the drying safe on its own?

Yes

With no stress, dried and damp worms survived about the same (89% against 97%). The drying itself is not what is killing them.

How sure can we be, with three runs? Every comparison above asks one thing: did the runs overlap? If the worst run in one group still beat the best run in the other, that is as strong as three runs can get. If they cross, the honest answer is that we could not tell them apart, not that they are the same.

A difference also has to be big enough to matter. A result here needs at least 10 percentage points, about five worms on a plate of fifty. Below that, three runs cannot tell a real effect from a good day.
