Version 3, end to end: microbes making soil in the bed itself

Version 3 makes soil in the dirt itself, with no tanks and no wash. Mars dust is poisonous and inorganic, so the same two things have to happen as always. The poison has to come out, and dead material and plant food have to go in. What is different is that nothing leaves the bed to have it done to it.

The shape of the whole thing

Before the stages, here is the map. It shows what goes into the bed, what the dirt is made of as the weeks pass, and what leaves.

Read it as three lanes. The things that go in run along the top: hydrogen the whole way, then each organism at the stage it is needed. The dirt runs through the middle, and it never moves: the perchlorate falls away to nothing, the chloride it leaves behind is blown off as a gas, and carbon and nitrogen build up in their place. The things that leave are underneath, and there is only one of them.

One box of damp dirt, with a hydrogen line going into it, run in three stages. Nothing is pumped anywhere. Nothing is washed. The same bed is the reactor, the growing bed and the finished soil. The stages are not three different machines. They are the same bed at three different times.

Why it is three stages and not one

Because the jobs fight each other when they happen together. There are three fights, and running the bed in order settles all three.

  • Making oxygen ruins nitrogen fixing. Pulling the poison apart releases oxygen inside the cell, and the enzyme that pulls nitrogen out of the air is wrecked by oxygen. So the poison is dealt with first, and the nitrogen after, and the two never meet.
  • Doing one job is cheaper than doing three. Bacteria that are only cleaning clears more poison per unit of hydrogen than one also trying to grow and fix nitrogen.
  • Worms and perchlorate should not meet. Nobody has ever measured how much perchlorate an earthworm can stand, so the worms go in last, when it is gone.

What the bed is given, in total

Hydrogen. That is the whole list.

Everything else comes off Mars: carbon dioxide and nitrogen from the air, water, and the salts and metals in the rock. The hydrogen is made by putting electricity through water, which a habitat already does.

Where the chlorine goes

Out as a gas, in stage 2. This is the part that lets the machinery go.

Destroying perchlorate leaves chloride, and chloride dissolves and stays. Version 2 gets rid of it by washing the dirt and carrying the water away. Version 3 has no wash, so a gene turns the chloride into methyl chloride, a gas that floats out of the bed by itself.

It is cleared slowly, over the whole of stage 2. There is no reason to hurry it, and hurrying it is expensive. Cleared at a slow, steady rate it costs about three per cent of the growth.
