Stage 1. Clean it

Azospira, a bacterium that lives on perchlorate, is put into the damp dirt with a hydrogen line. It takes the poison apart and breathes what comes off. Martian dirt holds nothing living for it to eat, so the bacterium works rather than breeds. That turns out to be the reason it cleans so well.

At a glance

Part of the stage What it is
In Martian regolith, damp. Hydrogen, piped in
Who does the work Azospira, a soil bacterium that already eats perchlorate for a living, in water treatment plants on Earth
What happens It prises the four oxygens off each perchlorate, spending hydrogen to do it, and breathes the oxygen it gets
Out Dirt with the poison gone. Chloride, left dissolved in the damp, waiting for stage 2

How it works

  • What perchlorate is. One chlorine atom with four oxygen atoms clamped onto it. To make it harmless, all four oxygens have to come off.
  • Taking oxygen off something takes hydrogen. That is what the hydrogen line is for. Put hydrogen and perchlorate together in water on their own, though, and nothing happens for a thousand years.
  • Azospira makes it happen. Its enzymes bring the two together, and the bacterium lives on the energy released. Hydrogen is the fuel. Azospira is the engine.
  • It breathes the oxygen it frees. The four oxygens come off as ordinary breathable oxygen, inside the cell, and that is what it breathes while it works. It needs no air.
  • The hydrogen ends up as water. It grabs the oxygen, not the chlorine. That is worth saying plainly, because it is the reason the chlorine is still a problem afterwards.

Why they work before they breed

This is the part that is easy to get backwards. Bacteria that are not multiplying clear more perchlorate, on less hydrogen, than bacteria trying to grow at the same time.

They are not being starved, and they do multiply. They do it in stage 2, once there is something in the bed to eat. Mars regolith arrives with nothing living in it, so there is nothing to grow on until the enriching has happened. Working first and breeding second is the order the material forces, and it happens to be the cheaper order too.

Perchlorate cleared Hydrogen spent on each
Bacteria that are not multiplying 5.81 6.88
Bacteria allowed to multiply and fix nitrogen 5.13 7.79
  • A cell that is building nothing only has to keep itself alive. Everything else it is given goes into taking the poison apart.
  • It needs no food and no nitrogen. In the model it cleared the whole batch with no carbon source and no fertiliser of any kind. It only needs feeding if it has to build new cells, and here it does not.
  • So stage 1 needs one organism and one pipe. No partner, nothing shipped in but hydrogen.

What is left behind

Chloride, and this is the honest catch. The poison is gone, but its chlorine is still in the bed, dissolved in the damp as the harmless half of table salt.

It stays there until stage 2. Nothing bad happens to it in the meantime, and it is dealt with by the microbe that arrives next, for a reason explained there.

Why it matters

  • This is the step that makes the dirt safe. Not fertile, not alive, just no longer poisonous. Everything after it is about making it grow things.
