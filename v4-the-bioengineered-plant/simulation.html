Version 4 in simulation

Before asking anyone for a bench, every design in this project is run through a computer model of its own chemistry, fed only what Mars has. Version 4 was held to the same rule. Here is what we used, what it said, and the one thing it turned out to be unable to answer.

What we used

A metabolic model is a list of every chemical reaction an organism can run. Give it a menu of food and it says whether the organism can grow, and how fast. It is not a living thing. It is a balance sheet.

About the model Detail
The model iRS1597, a published model of Arabidopsis thaliana, thale cress
Its size 1,576 reactions, 1,913 substances, 1,195 genes
Why this plant It is the plant version 4 would be designed in, and it is the only crop-like plant with a model good enough to use

This is the first version of the project that did not have to build its own model. Version 3 had to, because no model of its organism existed anywhere.

The Mars dirt menu

The rule is the same one version 2 wrote and version 3 reused. Close every way in, then reopen only what Mars can actually supply. Version 4 has one extra twist, because the plant stands in Martian dirt inside a human habitat, so the air is ours and the ground is Mars’s.

Where it comes from What
The habitat Carbon dioxide, oxygen, water, and light
Martian dirt Nitrate, sulfate, phosphate, and the metals in rock
Nowhere Ammonia, sugar, hydrogen sulfide

The published model is fed ammonia and hydrogen sulfide by default. Both are laboratory food. Leaving them open is exactly what made earlier versions of this project look healthier than they were, so both are closed.

Light is set to 43% of Earth daylight, because that is how much sunlight reaches Mars.

What it said

Martian dirt costs the plant about nine tenths of its growth

Growth
Laboratory medium, as published 8.9693
Martian dirt, with the chlorite dealt with 0.9812
Martian dirt, with unlimited nitrogen 3.8568

A plant can live in Martian dirt. It grows at roughly a ninth of the rate a well fed laboratory plant does, and most of that gap is nitrogen.

Nitrogen is what limits it, and nothing in the design makes more

Nitrate in the ground Growth
none cannot live
1 0.3271
3, roughly what Martian dirt holds 0.9812

unlimited 3.8568

Growth follows the nitrogen exactly, in a straight line, until the plant has all it can use. Every genetic trick in version 4 is beside the point if the nitrogen runs out, and Martian dirt holds a fixed amount of it.

The chlorite is not a setback. It is fatal

Growth
Nitrogen enzyme destroyed by chlorite cannot live

This is the number the whole design rests on. It is not a reduction in yield. With its nitrogen enzyme gone the plant cannot use nitrate at all, and nitrate is the only nitrogen Mars offers. The model says no growth of any kind.

One gene brings back every bit of it

Growth
The poisoned plant cannot live
The same plant with chlorite dismutase 0.9812

All of it. Adding the single bacterial enzyme returns the plant exactly to where it would be if Martian dirt had never held any chlorate at all.

And because Martian dirt does not ask permission, we forced the chlorite in at a fixed rate rather than letting the plant refuse it.

Chlorite forced on the plant Growth Oxygen released at the root
0 0.9812 0.00
1 0.9812 1.00
5 0.9812 5.00
20 0.9812 20.00

The plant carries the load without losing anything. Every unit of chlorite it disposes of hands it a unit of oxygen underground, and a root is always short of oxygen.

The checks it has to fail

A model that agrees with you is not evidence. A model that can make something out of nothing is worse than useless, and the earlier versions of this project were caught out twice by exactly that. Every one of these has to come back dead, and every one did.

The check Answer
Sealed off from everything cannot live
In the dark, with everything else on tap cannot live
No carbon dioxide cannot live
No nitrogen of any kind cannot live
Chlorite as the only thing on offer cannot live

What the model could not answer, and why we are saying so

It cannot test job 2 at all. Job 2 is the prising of phosphorus out of Martian rock, and we could not get the model to care about phosphorus in either direction. Tightening the supply to almost nothing changed no number anywhere.

Looking into why, the published model’s own recipe for building a plant gives off thirty units of phosphate for every unit of plant it builds, and takes none in from outside. Measured uptake from the ground is exactly zero at every setting we tried.

That is a flaw in the published model, not a finding about Mars. It means one of version 4’s three jobs cannot be checked this way, and has to be settled at a bench instead. We would rather say that than quietly report a number the model was never able to produce.

What no model of this kind can say

  • It has no clock. Nothing here says how long a radish takes. That has been the biggest gap since version 1 and it is still open.
  • It knows nothing about dirt. It models a cell, not a root pushing through grains with water films and dead ends in them.
  • It cannot say whether a borrowed protein folds. Whether a bacterial enzyme works inside a plant cell is a question for a bench, not a balance sheet.
  • It cannot see how much enzyme there is, only which reactions exist.
