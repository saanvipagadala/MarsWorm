The plant, the genes, and the question for each

Version 4 does not invent anything. Every gene below already works in some living thing on Earth today, doing the same job it is being asked to do here. That was version 1’s rule and it is still the rule.

Version 1 came to 37 genes and most of it did not survive testing. Version 4 comes to nine. Only two are new to plants, and one of those two is the hard part.

The plant itself

Two plants, for two different purposes, and it matters which is which.

Job 1: the gene that stops the self-poisoning

About the gene Detail
The gene cld, chlorite dismutase
From Azospira suillum, the same bacterium version 3 uses to clean dirt
What it does Splits chlorite into chloride and oxygen. Chloride is ordinary salt, which a plant can handle. The oxygen comes out at the root, and a root is always short of oxygen
Why it matters most A plant already makes chlorite by accident, out of the chlorate in Martian dirt. Bacteria survive that only because they carry this enzyme. Plants never have
Why it should work One protein. It needs no partner, it is well described, and it is already in version 3’s design
Nobody knows Whether it folds and works inside a plant cell. It has an iron heart, and the plant has to build that correctly

Job 1, the harder half: the genes for perchlorate

About the gene Detail
The genes pcrA and pcrB, perchlorate reductase
From The same bacterium
What they do Break perchlorate down to chlorate. After that the plant’s own enzyme and cld finish the job
Why they are needed Martian dirt holds perchlorate as well as chlorate, and the plant’s own enzyme only picks up the chlorate
Why they are the weak part They only work where there is no oxygen. They normally sit in a space between a bacterium’s two membranes that plants do not have. They also need a molybdenum helper delivered to them
Nobody knows Whether a bacterium’s breathing enzyme can work in a root that has oxygen in it

So version 4 comes in two sizes, and says so plainly.

Genes What it survives
The small version chlorite dismutase alone chlorate
The full version plus perchlorate reductase chlorate and perchlorate

If the answer to the question above is no, version 4 is the small version. The small version still has to be tested.

Job 2: the genes that unlock the rock

Martian dirt holds plenty of phosphorus and iron, locked in rock where a root cannot reach them. Plants already have a way of prising them out, and these genes turn it up.

Gene From What it does
SbMATE or HvAACT1 sorghum, barley Pushes acid out of the root, which frees phosphorus from rock
FRO2 thale cress Turns rust into iron a root can take in
IRT1 thale cress Carries that iron into the root
AHA2 thale cress Pumps acid around the root, so the other two work better
About the gene Detail
Why these are safe All four already do this job in living plants. Crops carrying stronger versions have grown on Earth for years, on acid soils and on soils that lock up iron
Nobody knows How much acid is enough, or what it costs. Making acid spends sugar the plant would otherwise grow with, and nobody has measured that trade in Martian material

Job 3: the genes that put the salt away

Splitting the chlorite makes chloride, right at the root, and Martian dirt holds more chloride to start with. A plant handles salt by locking it inside storage sacs in its cells, where it can do no harm.

Gene From What it does
AtNHX1 thale cress Pushes salt into the cell’s storage sac
AtCLCc thale cress Moves chloride into the same sac
About the gene Detail
Why it is not optional Job 1 makes salt on purpose. A design that deals with the chlorite and then dies of the salt has solved nothing
Nobody knows How much salt there is in the end. It depends on how much perchlorate and chlorate Martian dirt holds, and instruments in orbit cannot tell those two apart

The rule that shapes all of it

The rule What it means
The rule Every cleaning gene is driven by a switch that works only in the root
Why Perchlorate harms people by blocking the thyroid. A plant that stores it in the part you eat is poisonous, however well it grows
What that buys The perchlorate is destroyed where it comes in, and never travels to a leaf or a fruit
Nobody knows Whether it really stays in the root. This has to be measured before anybody eats anything, and it is the one result version 4 cannot publish without
