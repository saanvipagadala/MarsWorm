Job 1: stop the self-poisoning

Martian dirt holds two chlorine salts, perchlorate and chlorate. This page is about what those salts actually do to a plant, which is not what anyone assumes, and about the one gene that stops it.

What is in the dirt

What is in the dirt How much, and why
Perchlorate About half a percent of Martian dirt by weight. First measured by the Phoenix lander
Chlorate Present too, and probably in similar amounts. Instruments cannot cleanly tell it apart from perchlorate, so nobody knows the split
Why it is there Sunlight acting on chlorine in the dirt over a very long time

The usual explanation, and why it is not enough

The usual thing said about perchlorate is that it is toxic, so plants die. That is true and it explains nothing. Plenty of chemicals are toxic in ways plants shrug off, and perchlorate by itself is quite unreactive.

The real mechanism is stranger, and it is the plant’s own doing.

What actually happens

A plant takes nitrogen out of the ground as nitrate. To use it, it has an enzyme called nitrate reductase, whose job is to grab nitrate and convert it.

That enzyme cannot tell nitrate from chlorate. The two look alike to it. So in Martian dirt the enzyme grabs chlorate as well, and converts it, and what it makes is chlorite.

Chlorite is the dangerous one. It is far more reactive than the chlorate it came from, and the first thing it damages is the very enzyme that made it. The damage does not wear off.

So the sequence is this. The plant feeds on the chlorate by mistake. Feeding on it turns it into chlorite, which is far worse. That something then destroys the plant’s ability to use nitrate, and nitrate is the only nitrogen Martian dirt offers.

The plant does not simply meet a poison. It manufactures one, out of the harmless chlorate, using its own machinery. What it makes then disables the machine that made it.

Why bacteria do not have this problem

Bacteria that live on perchlorate make chlorite too. They make far more of it than a plant ever would, on purpose, because it is a step on the way to a meal.

They survive because they carry one extra enzyme, chlorite dismutase, which splits chlorite into two harmless things.

Chlorite goes in Chloride comes out and oxygen
the reactive one that does the damage ordinary salt, which a plant can handle useful, because roots are short of it

Plants do not have that enzyme. No plant has ever had it. That single absence is the whole reason Martian dirt is lethal to a plant and a feast to a bacterium.

The fix

Put the enzyme in the plant, in the root, and the chain breaks at the only point that matters. The plant still makes chlorite by accident. The chlorite is now split before it reaches anything important. The nitrogen enzyme survives, the plant keeps feeding, and the oxygen released lands exactly where a root wants it.

The gene that splits the chlorite →

The perchlorate is still there

The gene deals with chlorate, because chlorate is what the plant’s own enzyme activates. Perchlorate is a different matter. The plant’s enzyme largely ignores it, so it is never converted and never destroyed. It sits in the dirt, and the plant takes it up whole.

That does the plant no harm at all. It harms whoever eats the plant, which is why every cleaning gene in version 4 is kept down in the root, and why experiment 4 exists to find out whether it stays there.

Destroying the perchlorate, rather than carrying it around, would take a second pair of genes. Version 4 calls those its weakest part rather than pretending otherwise.

A route with no new gene at all

There is another way to break the same chain, at the other end. Instead of cleaning up the chlorite after the plant makes it, stop the plant making it, by editing its own nitrogen enzyme so it no longer accepts chlorate.

Researchers have already compared nitrate enzymes with perchlorate ones, and worked out which parts decide what gets accepted. So it would be a targeted edit and not a guess.

It would also mean no foreign DNA in the plant at all. Against that, it means editing an enzyme the plant cannot live without. That risk is why version 4 builds on the added gene instead, and keeps this as the route to try if the added gene fails.

What this job leaves behind

Chloride. Every chlorite split makes one, on top of the chloride Martian dirt already holds. Job 1 therefore creates the problem that job 3 exists to solve, and a design that ignored that would poison the plant with salt instead of with chlorite.
