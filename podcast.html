The podcast

This is the whole website, as a podcast. It is the quickest way to get the whole story, and the most enjoyable.

Could anything alive do an earthworm's job on Mars? Nothing on Earth can. So the project bioengineers an organism from 37 borrowed genes. Then it tests that organism in a computer simulation, under Martian conditions. Most of the design does not survive. So the project pivots. Version 2 uses ordinary Earth microbes and some plumbing. Three bacteria and a box of worms. Nothing bioengineered, and every piece of it exists today.

Twenty three minutes, five episodes, two hosts. Start at the first one. If you run a lab, 3 and 5 are the experiments you would be hosting.

  1. 1. The problem: Mars has dirt, not soil

    Why a colony cannot ship its food or its soil, and why the job on Earth belongs to the earthworm.

  2. 2. Version 1, the design: Building an organism from 37 borrowed genes

    Earth was searched first. The best organism on it managed 14 of the 23 abilities the job needs.

  3. 3. Version 1, the experiments: The two assumptions nobody has checked

    Fifteen cups of worms, and a roundworm with one gene switched off. What a lab would actually run.

  4. 4. Version 2, the pivot: The simulation that broke the design

    Fed only what Mars really has, the chosen organism could not live. A better idea came out of that.

  5. 5. Version 2, the experiments: Three questions for an earthworm

    Can a worm make soil out of regolith, how much perchlorate can it stand, and can it free phosphorus from solid rock? Three experiments, half a metre of shelf.
