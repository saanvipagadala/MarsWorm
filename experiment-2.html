Experiment 2: do genes carry abilities?

A design made of 37 borrowed genes assumes something nobody in this project has checked: that an ability travels with the gene behind it. This experiment checks it backwards, by breaking one gene in a living worm and seeing whether the ability goes with it.

This is the run sheet: the experiment written out exactly as it would be done at your bench, so a lab can see all of it, including the costs, before agreeing to host anything. The reasoning behind every choice in it is in Stage 4.

What we would be asking of you
The question Does drying protect C. elegans against Mars-like stress? And if it does, is one particular gene the reason?
Bench space Roughly 48 plates, and a shelf to keep them on
How long About four weeks
Biosafety BSL-1. No genetic modification is performed here: the knockout strains are ordered ready-made from a stock centre
From the lab A stereo microscope, a −20 °C freezer, NGM plates, OP50, worm picks, and standard C. elegans bench practice
From us The three strains, the UV-C lamp and relay, the humidity chamber, the simulant and the logger
The one thing only you can do The stock centre will not ship to a home address. An order needs an institutional address and a lab code

Why this experiment exists

The whole 37-gene design rests on one unchecked assumption. Put the gene in, get the ability. Take it out, lose it. This is the only place in the project where that assumption meets a living animal.

It tests one of the design’s 13 survival jobs, deeply: make the sugar that protects it while dry. One job tested properly is worth more than 13 tested shallowly, and most of the 13 could not be tested here anyway. No animal reduces perchlorate, or repairs radiation damage the way Deinococcus does.

Strains

All three are catalogue strains from the Caenorhabditis Genetics Center. Nothing is constructed.

What it is Strain Genotype Price
Wild type N2 wild isolate, Bristol $10
Drying gene #1 broken VC225 tps-1(ok373) X $10
Drying gene #2 broken RB760 tps-2(ok526) II $10

Both knockouts are deletion strains from the International C. elegans Gene Knockout Consortium. The worms are fed E. coli OP50.

The CGC does not ship to residential addresses. An order needs an institutional address and a lab code. The CGC assigns the code by email to cgc@umn.edu. This is the one thing a host institution is genuinely needed for.

Check WormBase before ordering whether tps-1 and tps-2 cover for each other. If they do, breaking one gene may show nothing at all. The CGC stocks no double mutant, so it would have to be crossed by hand.

The design

A plate of worms differs from any other plate in only three ways: dried or damp, normal or mutant, and which stress it met. All sixteen combinations are run, three times over, which is the 48 plates. Everything else is held identical, so a difference has nowhere else to come from.

The step What it means
1 Synchronise Grow a batch so every worm is the same age
2 Dry half of them Slowly, in a sealed humidity chamber over a saturated salt solution. The other half are kept damp
3 Stress both halves Each group meets one of three: a UV-C lamp, a −20 °C freeze, or MGS-1 dust. A fourth group gets no stress at all
4 Recover, then score 24 hours at room temperature, then alive or dead by touch response with a worm pick
5 Do all of it again With the mutant strain, whose drying gene is broken

Why a group gets no stress at all. Drying a worm out is rough treatment by itself. Without worms that are dried and then simply left alone, the drying killed them and the stress killed them look exactly the same in the data.

If drying protects, the dried worms survive better. If the gene is the reason, that advantage disappears in the mutant. Those are two different claims, and the mutant strain is the only way to tell them apart.

What this does and does not test

The stress Stands in for The honest gap
UV-C lamp radiation UV-C is not ionizing; Mars’s hazard is cosmic rays
−20 °C cold Mars averages about −60 °C
MGS-1 simulant dust Martian ground MGS-1 contains no perchlorate, so the actual poison is absent

So this tests survival on Martian dust with the poison left out. That is a much narrower claim than “survives Martian soil”, and it is the one being made.

If a strain does not arrive, decided in advance

Ordering takes about two weeks, and a strain can be delayed. If no mutant arrives in time, the experiment still runs: dried against damp on the wild type alone, which answers whether drying protects at all.

What it cannot answer then is the gene question, and the report says so rather than guessing. It prints “nothing to compare”, which the analysis handles explicitly. No part of the schedule depends on a strain arriving on time.

Recording rules: what goes on the page, and what does not

Four rules govern everything anyone writes down. Each one closes a way the experiment could quietly mislead whoever reads it later.

The rule What goes wrong without it
Write down two numbers per plate: worms on, worms alive at 24 h a sheet saying “62% survived” can never be checked, because the counts behind it are gone
A blank cell means not scored. Never type 0 a 0 says every worm on that plate died, which is a real result and a different claim
Score by touch response, never by “it looked still” a live worm can sit perfectly motionless, and gets recorded as dead
Plates coded before scoring whoever is at the microscope counts differently when they know which group they are holding

The sheets, blank and filled in

Two spreadsheets hold this experiment, filled in by hand at the bench and typed up exactly as written. Every column in them is defined in the codebook. They are empty, because nobody has run this yet.

The sheet What goes in it
plates.csv one row per plate: strain, dried or damp, which stress, worms started, worms alive at 24 hours
chamber_log.csv the drying chamber: hours, humidity in and out, temperature

The same two sheets also exist with numbers in them, so you can see what a finished run looks like before agreeing to produce one. Every number in them was invented by a script, written to test the analysis before there is any real data. This set is the hoped-for outcome, where drying protects and the gene is the reason.

What the sheets turn into

The analysis reads those two sheets and writes a report: charts, an answer to each of the three questions, and its own checks on the data. The rules that decide the answers were fixed before any data existed, so a disappointing result prints exactly as plainly as a good one.
